# Discovery Interview and Pilot Scorecard

**Use with:** `CANADIAN_EDUCATION_MARKET_DISCOVERY.md`  
**Status:** Research instrument — not approved product scope

## Research objective

Determine whether Canadian public colleges and polytechnics will buy a governed service-resolution layer that improves learner access and employee productivity with measurable financial and operational impact.

## Interview rule

Do not pitch first. Ask for a recent, specific example and reconstruct what actually happened. Separate observed facts from opinions and requested features.

## Executive interview

1. What are the three most important student-service or administrative efficiency priorities this year?
2. Which service outcomes are visible at executive or board level?
3. Where have budget pressure, staffing constraints, or enrolment changes forced operational trade-offs?
4. Which learner-service journeys create repeated complaints, delays, transfers, or equity concerns?
5. What AI or automation initiatives are active, stalled, or discontinued? Why?
6. Who owns the outcome, budget, technology, privacy, and operational change for one such journey?
7. What quantified result would justify a 90-day pilot and then a scaled purchase?
8. What would make the institution reject this category regardless of potential value?

## Service-owner interview

1. Walk through the last difficult enquiry that crossed departments.
2. Where did the learner start, and how many channels or people were involved?
3. Which information or policy was difficult to locate or interpret?
4. What percentage of enquiries are routine, ambiguous, sensitive, urgent, or personalized?
5. Where do transfers, repeat contacts, or incomplete referrals occur?
6. What data exists for volume, handle time, response time, resolution, and abandonment?
7. What knowledge sources are authoritative, who updates them, and how often do they conflict?
8. What may be automated, and what must remain a human decision?
9. What is the consequence of an incorrect answer or route?
10. Which result would materially improve the unit's budget or capacity?

## Front-line employee interview

1. Show how a normal enquiry is answered today.
2. Which systems, pages, documents, inboxes, or colleagues are consulted?
3. How much time is spent searching, interpreting, drafting, recording, and handing off?
4. Which questions recur most frequently?
5. Where do employees give different answers to the same question?
6. What work is delayed when enquiry volume rises?
7. How long does it take a new employee to become effective?
8. What kind of system suggestion would be trusted, checked, overridden, or ignored?
9. What information must never be entered into an AI tool?
10. What would make the workday measurably better?

## Learner interview

1. Tell the story of the last time help was needed from the institution.
2. How did the learner decide where to start?
3. Which words did the learner use versus the institution's terminology?
4. What was confusing, repetitive, inaccessible, or discouraging?
5. Was the correct service reached on the first attempt?
6. Was the next step clear, and was it completed?
7. What caused delay or abandonment?
8. When should digital self-service stop and a human take over?
9. What privacy, accessibility, language, or trust concerns matter?
10. What single improvement would have changed the outcome?

## Evidence capture

For every interview, record:

- Institution type, province, role, and service area.
- Recent incident and date range.
- User goal and triggering event.
- Channels and systems touched.
- Number of handoffs, repeat contacts, and elapsed time.
- Search, interpretation, and processing work.
- Failure or abandonment point.
- Human judgment and sensitive-data boundaries.
- Available baseline metrics.
- Budget owner and procurement path.
- Urgency and target timeframe.
- Exact outcome required for a paid pilot.

Do not store personally identifying student details in the research repository.

## Hypothesis scorecard

Score each institution from 0 to 3 on each dimension using observed evidence.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem frequency | Rare | Occasional | Weekly | Daily/high-volume |
| Operational impact | Negligible | Annoyance | Material workload | Budget/capacity consequence |
| Learner impact | Minor | Confusion | Delay/abandonment | Safety, equity, retention risk |
| Executive priority | None | General interest | Named initiative | Funded/urgent priority |
| Data availability | None | Anecdotal | Partial baseline | Reliable baseline and outcome data |
| Workflow suitability | High judgment | Mixed | Mostly informational | Governed, repeatable, measurable |
| Buyer ownership | Unknown | Multiple/no owner | Likely owner | Named budget owner |
| Pilot feasibility | Blocked | More than 6 months | 3–6 months | 60–90 days |
| Governance fit | Unacceptable | Major unknowns | Manageable review | Clear approved path |
| Expansion potential | One-off | One department | Multiple services | Institution-wide reusable capability |

**Interpretation:**

- 24–30: strong design-partner candidate.
- 18–23: promising; resolve buyer, data, or governance gap.
- 12–17: continue discovery; do not build institution-specific features.
- Below 12: deprioritize.

A high score is not approval to sell; it is evidence for a structured pilot discussion.

## Baseline metrics

| Metric | Definition | Baseline source | Pilot direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contact volume | Enquiries per channel and reason | CRM/contact centre/email | Context, not automatically lower |
| Avoidable contact rate | Routine enquiries safely resolved without staff | Sample audit | Down in staff-handled volume |
| Average handle time | Active staff time per enquiry | Contact centre/time study | Down |
| After-contact work | Recording and follow-up time | Time study | Down |
| First-contact resolution | Resolved without transfer/repeat contact | CRM/sample audit | Up |
| Transfer rate | Enquiries reassigned to another unit | CRM/ticket logs | Down |
| Repeat-contact rate | Same need contacts again in defined window | De-identified linkage | Down |
| Successful referral | Learner reaches correct service/next step | Referral confirmation | Up |
| Process completion | Required next action completed | Workflow aggregate | Up |
| Time to resolution | Elapsed time from initial contact to outcome | CRM/workflow | Down |
| Retrieval top-1/top-3 accuracy | Correct approved source ranking | Golden questions | Up/within threshold |
| Escalation accuracy | Correct trigger and human destination | Golden questions/human review | Meet safety threshold |
| Staff override rate | Recommendation changed or rejected | Product telemetry | Explainable and monitored |
| Learner effort | Reported ease/steps/repetition | Short survey | Improve |
| Cost per resolved enquiry | Allocated labour and platform cost / resolved needs | Finance + operations | Down |

## P&L worksheet

Keep value categories separate:

### Cash savings

- Reduced overtime.
- Reduced contractor or vendor expenditure.
- Eliminated overlapping software.
- Actual vacancy or staffing-plan change.

### Capacity release

- Hours redirected to higher-value service.
- Reduced search, handling, and rework time.
- Faster employee onboarding.

### Cost avoidance

- Future hires not required for demand growth.
- Reduced need for additional service capacity.

### Revenue or retention protection

- Improved completion of enrolment, fee, aid, accessibility, or support processes.
- Retention claims only where study design supports attribution.

### Formula

`Annual operational value = avoided contact cost + handling-time capacity value + avoided rework + verified cost avoidance − annual platform and operating cost`

Do not convert capacity into cash savings unless the budget changes.

## 90-day pilot gates

### Safety gate

- Approved sources and named content owners.
- Privacy, security, accessibility, language, and records review.
- No autonomous high-stakes decisions.
- Human escalation owners and tested routing.
- Golden-question and adversarial evaluation thresholds.
- Incident, correction, rollback, and audit process.

### Business-value gate

- Baseline available before launch.
- Target improvement agreed with budget owner.
- Adoption and workflow-change plan agreed.
- Costs captured completely.
- Results separated into cash, capacity, avoidance, and revenue/retention.
- Scale decision uses measured outcomes, not demo quality alone.

## Competitive interview prompts

1. What tools are currently used for search, chatbot, CRM, contact centre, knowledge management, advising, workflow, and enterprise AI?
2. Which problem was each tool purchased to solve?
3. What has and has not worked?
4. Where does the current stack stop: answer, route, handoff, completion, analytics, or governance?
5. What is the cost and renewal date?
6. What integration, data residency, accessibility, bilingual, or procurement constraints apply?
7. Would this platform complement, consolidate, or duplicate the current stack?

## Go/no-go rules

Proceed to a design-partner offer only when:

- Three or more institutions independently confirm the same pain.
- A named executive owns a measurable outcome and budget.
- Baseline operational data exists.
- A 60–90 day pilot can change and measure the workflow.
- Governance requirements can be satisfied.
- The proposed differentiation survives the competitive scan.

Pivot from learner navigation to employee assist if productivity evidence is materially stronger, while retaining the governed service graph. Do not claim an institution-wide AI platform until multiple deployed workflows reuse the same knowledge, policy, evaluation, and governance capabilities.
