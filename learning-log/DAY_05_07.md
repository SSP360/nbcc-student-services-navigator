## Gate 1 — Evidence Inventory: What Was Found

Inspected the existing source catalogue, curated content, retrieval/routing/escalation
code, tests, and prior learning logs before producing anything new. This surfaced one
previously-untracked, material finding, verified by directly running the real pipeline
(not assumed):

**Only 3 of 7 approved sources are curated.** `NBCC-SS-004` (accessibility) and
`NBCC-SS-006` (financial support) are approved in `knowledge/sources.yaml` but have no
curated content in `knowledge/curated/`. This means the `accessibility_inclusion` and
`financial_support` journeys — 2 of the 5 canonical journeys this increment was asked to
demonstrate — cannot resolve correctly end-to-end today. Verified directly:

```
Query: "How do I apply for a student loan or financial aid?"
  Top retrieval result: NBCC-SS-002 (academic_support) — wrong
  Journey (actual): academic_support — wrong, should be financial_support

Query: "I need accommodations for my disability, who do I contact?"
  Top retrieval result: NBCC-SS-005 (wellbeing) — wrong
  Journey (actual): wellbeing_safety — wrong, should be accessibility_inclusion
```

However, the **routing logic itself** was separately verified correct for both journeys
via existing, already-passing unit tests (`tests/routing.test.ts`: "maps accessibility
domain to accessibility_inclusion journey", "maps financial_support domain to
financial_support journey") — this is a content-coverage gap, not a routing-logic defect.

A second, more encouraging finding: the **escalation layer's `accommodation_request`
trigger** correctly identifies and routes accessibility-related requests to `NBCC-SS-004`
even without curated content, because its target is hardcoded independent of
journey/retrieval (the same safety pattern used for `crisis_or_safety` since Day 3). A
third, disclosed finding: the `personalized_decision` trigger (relevant to financial
decision language) does **not** have this hardcoded protection — its target is
journey-derived, so it can misroute for financial queries today. All three findings are
documented in full, with exact command output, in
`docs/design-partner-readiness/service-resolution-traces.md` (Traces 4–5) rather than
smoothed over to present a cleaner five-for-five story.

## New Limitations Surfaced by This Increment

1. **Two of seven approved sources remain uncurated**, leaving `accessibility_inclusion`
   and `financial_support` without end-to-end retrieval evidence (see Gate 1 above and
   `service-resolution-traces.md`). Recorded as `D-FUTURE-01` in `product/BACKLOG.md`.
2. **`personalized_decision`'s escalation target is not hardcoded** the way
   `crisis_or_safety` and `accommodation_request` are — for financial-decision-phrased
   queries today, this means an escalation can fire but route to the wrong contact
   (inherits whatever journey the content gap in #1 causes). Whether to hardcode this
   trigger's target per-domain once financial content exists is a design question raised,
   not answered, in `service-resolution-traces.md` (Trace 5).
3. **No real-data or live-workflow validation exists for any of this package's claims** —
   every trace, measurement design, and pilot charter in this increment is built and
   reasoned about, not tested against a real institution, real staff, or real students.
   This is the central, honest gap the proposed pilot charter (`pilot-charter.md`) exists
   to close, not something this increment resolves.
